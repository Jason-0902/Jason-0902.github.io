Research

Autoware Fuzzing for Autonomous Driving Systems

Undergraduate Research Project · Chung Yuan Christian University · December 2023 – April 2024

Research Advisor: Dr. Hsiao-Rong Tyan

Overview

Investigated the feasibility of applying fuzz testing techniques to Autoware, an open-source autonomous driving platform.

The project focused on understanding the technical challenges of testing complex ROS 2-based software components, including API testability, software dependencies, and fuzzing integration.

Research activities

  • Explored the feasibility of fuzz testing Autoware, examining API testability, ROS 2 integration, and build dependencies.
  • Conducted libFuzzer experiments on a motion_utils deceleration-distance function using a harness that converted generated inputs into six velocity, acceleration, and jerk parameters.
  • Investigated Autoware APIs and assessed input requirements and integration constraints for developing a custom fuzzer.
  • Used AddressSanitizer (ASan) to examine memory error reports in Autoware C++ components.
  • Collaborated on AFL++ experiments as part of a research team.
  • Configured and ran DriveFuzz on laboratory hardware, troubleshooting GPU driver and virtualization issues.
  • Encountered computing resource and runtime constraints that limited full reproduction of previously reported DriveFuzz results.

Outcome

Developed practical experience in configuring and evaluating fuzzing workflows for complex software systems, identifying integration challenges and studying the feasibility of applying fuzzing techniques to autonomous driving software.
